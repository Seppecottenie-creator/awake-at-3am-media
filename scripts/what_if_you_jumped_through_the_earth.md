# What if you jumped through the Earth?

Short, 9:16, 49.5 s (v2, with Ollie). Video: `shorts/what_if_you_jumped_through_the_earth_v2.mp4`
(v1, 85 s with the old boy: `shorts/what_if_you_jumped_through_the_earth.mp4`, to be removed once the scheduled post points to v2.)

Hook: Ollie's bedroom at night; the floor breaks open under him and he drops into the hole, question big on screen.
Ending: Ollie shivering in his pyjamas at the North Pole (`shiver` clip).

## Script

1. What if your floor opened up, and you fell straight through the Earth? Spoiler: it's quicker than a movie.
2. Our deepest hole ever is just over twelve kilometers. Zero point two percent of the way to the center. Then the rock got too hot.
3. So, magic tunnel. No air, heatproof walls. Handy, because the inner core is about as hot as the Sun's surface.
4. You fall faster and faster, weightless. By the center: eight kilometers per second. Way faster than a rifle bullet.
5. There, gravity pulls equally from every side. But you're so fast, you zoom straight through.
6. Then gravity slows you down, and you reach the other side just as you stop. About thirty-eight minutes.
7. Grab the edge! Or you'll yo-yo between the poles forever.
8. One catch: the Earth spins. Start anywhere but a pole, and you slam into the wall. So, Ollie: move to the North Pole. Pack a jacket.

## Fact check

| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Deepest hole ever drilled: Kola Superdeep Borehole, 12,262 m; ~180 °C at the bottom (about 100 °C was expected), heat stopped the drilling | [Wikipedia: Kola Superdeep Borehole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kola_Superdeep_Borehole), [Futura Sciences](https://www.futura-sciences.com/en/?p=29583) |
| 12.262 km / 6,371 km ≈ 0.19 % of the way to the center | calculation (mean Earth radius 6,371 km) |
| Inner core roughly 5,000–6,000 °C, comparable to the Sun's surface (~5,500 °C) | [Wikipedia: Earth's inner core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth%27s_inner_core), [Gizmodo on Anzellini et al., Science 2013](https://gizmodo.com/earth-s-inner-core-is-as-hot-as-the-surface-of-the-sun-482354178), [Nature 363, 534](https://dx.doi.org/10.1038/363534a0) |
| Pole-to-pole fall takes ~38 min with a realistic density model (42 min for uniform density) | Klotz, *Am. J. Phys.* 83, 231 (2015), [arXiv:1308.1342](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.1342), [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/50312-how-long-to-fall-through-earth.html), [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2015-03-fall-hole-earth.pdf) |
| Peak speed at the center around 8 km/s | Klotz quoted in [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/50312-how-long-to-fall-through-earth.html); uniform-density value √(gR) ≈ 7.9 km/s |
| Rifle bullet ~1 km/s | typical rifle muzzle velocity 0.8–1.2 km/s |
| Weightless in free fall; zero net gravity at the center; without air you oscillate forever | standard Newtonian mechanics (shell theorem, simple harmonic motion) |
| Earth's rotation (Coriolis) pushes you into the wall unless the tunnel runs along the spin axis | [Physics Forums discussion](https://www.physicsforums.com/threads/what-would-happen-if-you-drilled-to-the-other-end-of-the-planet.658568/); standard rotating-frame mechanics |
| Equator spins at about 1,670 km/h | 40,075 km circumference / 24 h |

Note: in the Coriolis shot the planet's spin is sped up so the effect is visible.
