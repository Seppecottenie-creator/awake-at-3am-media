# What if the Sun disappeared?

Short, 9:16, 86 s. Video: `shorts/what_if_the_sun_disappeared.mp4`

## Script

1. What if the Sun just... disappeared? Good news: you wouldn't notice right away. For about eight minutes, everything seems fine.
2. Sunlight needs about eight minutes and twenty seconds to reach us. So the light already on its way keeps shining, like nothing happened.
3. Gravity also travels at the speed of light. So for those same eight minutes, Earth keeps circling an empty spot. Like a dog chasing a ball you only pretended to throw.
4. Then the lights go out. The Moon goes dark too, since moonlight is just reflected sunlight. And Earth flies off in a straight line, at about thirty kilometers per second.
5. Weirdly, Jupiter would keep shining for more than an hour. Its light is just running late.
6. Then it gets cold. Within a week, the average temperature drops below freezing. And it keeps falling for years.
7. Without sunlight, plants can't make food. And almost everything else on the surface depends on plants.
8. But not everything. Oceans freeze from the top down, and the ice works like a blanket. Deep down at hot vents, microbes live on chemical energy, not sunlight. They might not even notice.
9. Relax, though. The Sun can't just vanish. It has about five billion years of fuel left. So yes, you still need sunscreen.

| Earth's orbital speed ≈ 30 km/s (29.8 km/s) | NASA Earth fact sheet |
| Jupiter keeps shining for over an hour after we see the Sun vanish | calculation: at opposition Sun→Jupiter ≥ 4.95 AU (≥ 41 light-min) + Jupiter→Earth ≥ 3.95 AU (≥ 33 light-min) ≈ 74 min, minus 8.3 min for the Sun ≈ 66 min minimum |
