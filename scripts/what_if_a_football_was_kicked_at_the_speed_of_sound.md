## Fact check

| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Fastest football kick by a machine: 225 km/h | [Guinness World Records](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/fastest-football-kick-by-a-machine) |
| Speed of sound at sea level on a standard day: 761 mph ≈ 1,225 km/h ≈ 340 m/s | [NASA Glenn: Speed of Sound](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/WWW/k-12/BGP/sound.html) |
| Foot-ball contact in an instep kick: about 9 ms | Instep kick biomechanics literature, e.g. [Nunome et al. 2006](https://people.stfx.ca/smackenz/courses/HK474/Midterm/Nunome%202006%20Impact%20phase%20kinematics%20of%20instep%20kicking%20in%20soccer.pdf), [ISBS proceedings](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/5637/5130) |
| Real shots peak around 2,000 N (on-screen card) | Asai et al. 2002 (2,439 N), Ishii et al. 2007 (over 2,000 N at 24 m/s), via [ISBS proceedings](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/5153/4730) |
| Ball mass 410–450 g | [IFAB Laws of the Game, Law 2](https://www.theifab.com/laws/chapter/22/section/32) |
| About 15,000 N of push | calculation: Δp = 0.43 kg × 340 m/s = 146 N·s; 146 / 0.010 s = 14.6 kN average over 10 ms |

| Penalty distance 11 m; about 40 ms to reach the goal | IFAB Laws of the Game, Law 1 (penalty mark 11 m from the goal line); calculation with drag: t = (e^(kx) − 1)/(k·v₀) gives 38–43 ms for Cd 0.5–0.9 (32 ms without drag) |
| A keeper's visual reaction is about 200 ms (measured 200–300 ms for simple responses, more for a full dive) | [NeuroTrackerX](https://www.neurotrackerx.com/post/the-science-of-penalty-kicks-what-goalkeepers-see-before-the-ball-moves), [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2009/12/091209193325.htm) |

| Energy of about 25,000 J, the same as a 1.5-tonne car at about 20 km/h | calculation: ½ × 0.43 × 340² = 24.9 kJ; v = √(2 × 24,900 / 1,500) = 5.76 m/s = 20.7 km/h |

## Simplifications

- The 15,000 N assumes the normal 10 ms contact time. A real kick at this speed would be shorter and even more violent, so the number is a lower-end estimate.
- The car comparison is for the energy leaving the foot; the ball has lost a good part of it by the time it reaches the goal.
- The hardest human shots are left out because the figures conflict between sources (129 km/h is the Guinness-listed record, 211 km/h is a widely repeated but unverified claim).
